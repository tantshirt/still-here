# PROJECT BRIEF
## STILL HERE

> The original brief, written 2026-09-23. Some details changed during design; the final threshold copy is in the [README](../README.md).

A one-page art piece about being alive right now.  
Two screens. Then nowhere else to go.

---

### What this is

Someone opens a site. Black. A short message that lands hard enough they have to press Enter. Then they are under a floating slab of light. Standing figures cover it. New people are placed onto it from above. Others lose their light and fall through the floor.

That is the whole website. No about. No menu that leads away. No second act after the slab.

The name is **STILL HERE**. After a fall it changes key: the dead are not. You are. That is the hit.

---

### What it should do to a person

Not teach population.
Not make them sad on purpose.
Not send them into the past or the future.

It should pierce.

Every second, someone is arriving and someone is leaving. The person looking is on the slab. They should leave the tab more in their own life than they were before — call someone, put the phone down, enjoy what they already have.

Past and future are glances. The home of the piece is **this second**.

---

### The feeling

Cold-holy. Monumental. Quietly confrontational.

One image, shot from underneath, industrial and almost religious. A single object in the dark. Everything else is weather happening to it.

Not a dashboard. Not a clock. Not a charity film. If it looks like a product, it failed. If it looks like a photograph of an installation nobody was allowed to film, it worked.

---

### Screen 1 — the threshold

Black. Almost nothing.

Type, small, centered or slightly high of center. White, or a white that is barely there until it finishes arriving. No logo. No `Welcome to Still Here`. No UI chrome.

They read. They press **Enter**.

That press is the whole funnel: the line has to be good enough that not pressing feels like leaving a sentence unfinished. Enter also unlocks sound on the next screen. The browser needs a gesture anyway — make the gesture mean something.

**Do not** explain the project on this screen. Do not say births, deaths, data, world, visualization. If they already understand the artwork, the slab cannot hit.

**Do not** make them click a button that looks like a button. The word `ENTER` can sit alone, very small, under the text, or not exist until they pause. Keyboard and tap do the same thing.

If there is motion, it is only type arriving. One line at a time. Slow enough to be read at full weight. No ken burns, no particles, no slab peeking through. The first image they earn is the congregation.

### Threshold copy

**DECIDED (2026-09-23): Option 1 — the strike (Marcus Aurelius).** This is the only threshold copy. Options 2–5 below are kept for reference only and are not to be used.

Pick **one**. Do not rotate. Do not stack them.

**1 — the strike (recommended)**
```
You could leave life right now.

Let that determine
what you do and say and think.
```
Marcus Aurelius. Already a blade. Do not attribute on this screen. Credit later, in the quiet colophon, if at all.

**2 — the name, used as a sentence**
```
Some of us just got here.
Some of us are already gone.

You are still here.
```
Then Enter. The title becomes the last line they read before the slab.

**3 — the thesis, said once**
```
You cannot stand in the past.
You cannot stand in the future.

This is the part that is yours.
```

**4 — colder, shorter**
```
The dead are not here.

You are.
```

**5 — if you want them slightly unsteady**
```
Right now
someone is arriving.

Right now
someone is leaving.

You are between those two facts.
```
This one flirts with explaining. Only use it if 1–4 feel too abstract. Still do not say “birth” or “death.”

**Stay away from**
Anything that sounds like a wellness brand, a tattoo, or a commencement speech. No “live laugh,” no “make it count,” no “life is short so chase your dreams.” If it would work on a tote bag, it is not the door to this piece.

**How the type should feel**
One voice. Tight leading. Plenty of black around it. If you animate, each stanza holds. The last line stays a beat longer than is comfortable. Then `ENTER` appears like an afterthought — or they discover that Enter works without being told.

After Enter: cut to black for a breath, then Screen 2. Not a dissolve that previews the 3D. A cut.

---

### Screen 2 — the piece

Black.
The slab is already there, already occupied — or it drops into frame in one move.
Two downlights. One fall.
A caption, very late, very small:

`THE WORLD  ·  NOW`

If they do nothing, it continues. That is the point.

---

### The object

One slab. Always the same slab.

Proportions of a thin floating platform: raw light for a floor, dark edges, cables and rig visible in the black above. Do not hide the engineering. The float is the religion.

Under it: a pit with no floor. Haze. Fallen figures dissolve before they could land. You never see impact. If the pit becomes a destination, the piece becomes cruel instead of sacred.

**The slab does not change shape for countries or years.** Japan and Nigeria, 1950 and 2085, are the same altar. What changes is how full it is.

- Sparse: air between bodies. You can count.
- Packed: a floor of people. The slab reads as one glowing mass.
- Overfull: they stack. Density becomes architecture.

World-now should feel slightly too full — a house that keeps receiving guests.

---

### The people

Flat glowing silhouettes. No faces, no clothes, no age, no gender. Not babies. A birth here is a person being placed into the living.

They take one step after they arrive. Then they stand. A standing crowd is a choir. A walking crowd is a game.

Most of them are climate. A few are noticed: a longer downlight, a fall closer to the camera. That is enough intimacy.

---

### Birth

A hard white downlight pins a point on the slab.
A silhouette is already standing in it.
It takes one step into the pack.
The downlight dies.

Never: cribs, crying, storks, particles assembling a human.

---

### Death

A figure’s light goes out while it is still standing.
The floor stops holding it.
It falls a short way, slowly, without flail.
It disappears in the haze.

Never: graves, red, screams, a body hitting anything.

---

### The rate (the argument you don’t have to label)

Around now, worldwide:

- about **4 people born every second**
- about **2 people dying every second**
- the slab **gains** people

Nobody needs a caption to understand the world is still filling. That visible surplus is the piece being honest. It is also why the message is not “everything is ending.” It is “you are here, in the middle of it.”

At world speed, do not try to make six stories a second. Arrivals are weather on top of the slab. Falls are weather underneath. The eye reads two rates against each other.

When they filter down to one country, events become sentences. Japan has stillness between a placement and a fall. Other places never rest. A small place may hold one figure alone. Do not fake density to make composition prettier.

---

### Camera

Three positions. That is the whole film language.

- **Under** — the default, the poster, the hit. Slab as ceiling. You occupy the pit. Births punch through as downlights. Deaths pass you in the air.
- **Level** — the island. Thickness, cables, the edge. For sparse places. For watching one marked figure.
- **Above** — the slab as a single rectangle of living matter in black. Use it. Don’t live here. Stay too long and it becomes a chart.

The slab may drift on its cables, slow as breathing. No joyride orbit.

---

### The page

Screen 1, then Screen 2. That is the entire website.

What may exist *on* Screen 2, as part of the piece:

- The congregation
- A thin caption (place + now/year)
- Filters, hidden until they reach for them
- Sound, unlocked by the Enter from Screen 1
- One marked figure, if they ask
- One saying, at the end of looking — not another page

What does not exist:

- Other routes
- Blog, shop, donate, newsletter
- A map to click
- Live counters on the default view
- Explainer paragraphs over the slab
- A replay of the poem unless they refresh

One sentence may appear after they have been under the slab a moment, then recede:

*Each placement is a birth. Each fall is a death.*

That is the whole manual. The threshold already did the soul work. Do not preach twice.

---

### Filters (looking closer, not leaving)

When someone reaches for controls, they are still in the same room.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| Place | Same slab, different occupancy and rate. World / region / country. |
| When | Defaults to **now**. Other years are a glance. After a while it should feel right to come back to now. |
| Born / gone / both | Pit off, or downlights off. Births-only is a miracle that will not stop. Deaths-only is a congregation thinning. |
| Speed | Slow is rite. Real-time is climate. Fast is the year as weather. |
| Camera | Under / level / above. |
| One life | A single figure stays slightly brighter. Not “you.” Attention, attached to one body in the mass. When it falls, the mark can go out. That should sting a little, then the slab continues. |

Do not add sex, age, cause, GDP, religion. Those turn the altar into a report.

Year is the dangerous filter. This piece is about the present. Past and future are allowed because the data can show a sparse 1950 or a packed-then-hesitating late century — but the site should always treat **now** as home.

---

### Sound

A bed, not a song.

After Enter: the hum of a rig in a large room. Births as a soft placement, like a light switch. Deaths as a duller drop, felt more than heard. At world rate they become texture. At country rate they separate.

Mute must work. Silence should still be the piece. No soundtrack under the poem. The threshold is typography. The slab is the first sound.

---

### How presence is built

The data is only the score. The meaning is:

You cannot stand on the past. You cannot stand on the future. You can only stand on the slab.

So:

- Default state after Enter is **the world, this second**.
- No countdown, no “births today so far,” no scoreboard. Counting is how you leave the present.
- The marked figure is the only personal mechanic — one body, still here.
- Speed-up exists so they can feel a year, then drop back to 1× and notice how a single second lands.
- The saying on Screen 1 is the strike. Anything on Screen 2 is an echo, not a second sermon.

The hit is not “death is coming.” The hit is “you are among the ones still standing.”

---

### The saying on the slab (echo only)

Do not put a new quote over the image at the start. They already walked through one.

If something returns after they have watched, keep it small and rare:

- **Still here.**
- **This is the part that is yours.**
- **The present is all we have.**

One voice with Screen 1. If Screen 1 is Marcus, do not switch to Ram Dass on the slab. Commit.

---

### More lines to sit with (not for the site unless you replace Screen 1)

- “Think of yourself as dead. You have lived your life. Now take what’s left and live it properly.” — Marcus Aurelius
- “Let us postpone nothing.” — Seneca
- “Some things are rushing into existence, others out of it.” — Marcus Aurelius  
  (Almost a description of the artwork. Better as a later echo than a door.)
- “Death twitches my ear. ‘Live,’ he says. ‘I am coming.’” — Virgil
- “We’re all just walking each other home.” — Ram Dass  
  (Softer. Only if the falls feel too cold and you want a hand on the shoulder.)
- “While you’re alive and able — be good.” — Marcus Aurelius

---

### Data (only what the art needs)

Yearly births and deaths, spread across the seconds of that year. There are no real-time individual events anywhere. The “live” feeling is the year performed as a metronome.

For *now* plus other years, UN World Population Prospects is the source that can do 1950 through projections to 2100. Cache the numbers. Credit them once, in a colophon so quiet it is almost not there. Never on the slab. Never on Screen 1.

Visible bodies are not a census. A few thousand standing stamps, packing and stacking as occupancy rises. Net growth should still be felt.

---

### Laws

1. Two screens. A threshold, then the slab. Nowhere else.
2. Screen 1 does not explain. Screen 2 does not preach.
3. One slab. Never rebuilt into a map, a chart, or a country outline.
4. No faces.
5. Births from above. Deaths through the floor. Nothing else happens to a body.
6. No ground in the pit.
7. At world speed, climate — not six readable stories a second.
8. The interface is a caption, not a cockpit.
9. Default is now.
10. The name is **STILL HERE**. Use it like a sentence, not a brand.

---

### What success looks like

They read the first screen and need the next one.
They press Enter.
They understand the slab without copy.
They stay longer than they meant to.
They screenshot the underside.
They change place once, just to see a quiet country go still.
They close the tab and are slightly more in their life than they were before.

If they leave thinking about statistics, the piece hid behind the data.
If they leave thinking about death only, it leaned too far into the pit.
If they leave thinking *I’m still here* — that’s the brief.
